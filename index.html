    [CLAIMS]

    Storm damage & insurance

    How homeowner's claims for roof and exterior damage actually work, when filing makes sense, and where I fit in. Plain English, no storm-chaser games.

    USUALLY WORTH A CLAIM
    • Hail damage. Bruised or cracked shingles, dents in gutters, vents, downspouts, window wraps and AC fins. Dented soft metals are often the first proof hail hit your property.
    • Wind damage. Missing, creased or lifted shingles, torn ridge cap, siding blown off.
    • A fallen tree or limb that punctured the roof or damaged siding, gutters or windows.
    • A sudden leak from storm damage that's now staining ceilings or walls inside.
    • Damage well above your deductible, reported within your policy's deadline. Many policies give you a year or less from the date of the storm.
    THINK TWICE / SKIP IT
    • Age and wear. Curling, granule loss from age, and a roof at the end of its life are maintenance, not covered damage.
    • Damage close to your deductible. If the repair is $2,400 and your deductible is $2,000, the claim pays little and still goes on your record.
    • Small repairs like a few blown-off shingles or a loose gutter. Paying out of pocket protects your claims history.
    • Pre-existing or neglected issues: old leaks, clogged gutters, failed flashing that was never maintained.
    • Several recent claims. Multiple claims in a few years can raise premiums or affect renewal. Ask your agent how your carrier handles it.

    WORDS ON YOUR PAPERWORK

    Deductible
    What you pay before insurance pays. Wind/hail deductibles are often a percentage of your dwelling coverage: 2% of $350,000 is $7,000.
    RCV (replacement cost value)
    Pays to replace with new materials. The first check usually holds back depreciation; you get it once the work is finished and invoiced.
    ACV (actual cash value)
    Pays the depreciated value of your old roof. On an older roof that can be a fraction of the replacement cost.
    Recoverable depreciation
    The amount held back on an RCV policy. It's your money, but you have to finish the job and submit the final invoice, usually within a set deadline.
    Scope of loss
    The adjuster's line-item estimate of what's damaged and what it pays for. Read every line.
    Supplement
    A request to add items the original estimate missed, like drip edge, ice & water shield, starter strip or code-required work, backed by photos and measurements.
    Code upgrade (ordinance or law)
    Optional coverage that pays for work current building code requires, even if your old roof didn't have it. Check if you have it.
    Two-party check
    If you have a mortgage, checks are often made out to you and your lender, who has to endorse them before funds are released.

    HOW A CLAIM GOES, START TO FINISH

    1. 01

      Stay safe, document, protect

      Note the storm date. Take photos from the ground, not the roof. Stop further damage with a tarp or by moving belongings. Reasonable emergency repairs are usually reimbursable, so keep receipts.

      ME: Emergency tarp and a photo record of everything.

    2. 02

      Free inspection before you file

      A professional looks at the roof, gutters, soft metals, siding and windows and tells you honestly whether the damage is storm-related and likely above your deductible.

      ME: Full inspection with a photo report and a straight answer, even if the answer is "don't file."

    3. 03

      You file the claim

      Call your insurer or use their app. Write down your claim number, adjuster name and any deadlines they give you.

      ME: I'll walk you through what to say and what to send.

    4. 04

      Adjuster inspection

      The insurer sends an adjuster to inspect. Having your contractor there makes sure damage isn't missed.

      ME: With your OK, I meet the adjuster on the roof and point out every hit.

    5. 05

      Review the estimate

      Compare the adjuster's scope to a contractor's scope line by line. Missed items get documented and submitted as a supplement. If you still disagree, ask for a re-inspection, and your policy's appraisal clause or a licensed public adjuster are options.

      ME: Line-by-line comparison plus supplement documentation for missed items and code work.

    6. 06

      First check arrives

      On an RCV policy, usually the actual cash value minus your deductible. Mortgage companies may need to endorse it first.

    7. 07

      Choose materials and build

      Pick your products and colors, schedule the crew, and get it done right. Insurance pays to restore; upgrades beyond that are your choice and your cost.

      ME: Design in the visualizer, order materials, manage the crew start to finish.

    8. 08

      Close it out

      Submit the final invoice and completion photos to release the held-back depreciation. You pay your deductible to the contractor, the same as any repair.

      ME: Final invoice, photos and paperwork so your depreciation gets released.

    WHAT I DO FOR YOU✓
    • Free storm inspection with a photo report
    • An honest file-or-don't recommendation
    • Meet your adjuster on site
    • A detailed line-item estimate for your claim
    • Supplement documentation for missed and code-required items
    • Manage the build and the final paperwork to close the claim
    WHAT I WON'T DO✕
    • Waive, absorb or "cover" your deductible. It's illegal in many states and can void a claim.
    • Negotiate your settlement for you. In most states only licensed public adjusters or attorneys can.
    • Ask you to sign over your insurance benefits on the spot.
    • Inflate damage or bill for work that wasn't done.
    STORM-CHASER RED FLAGS!
    • Door-knockers right after a storm with out-of-area plates
    • "Free roof" or "we'll cover your deductible"
    • Pressure to sign a contract before an inspection or adjuster visit
    • Large cash deposits up front
    • No local address, license or references

    Had a storm? Get me involved early.

    The best time to call is before you file. I'll inspect, tell you straight whether it's worth a claim, and if it is, I'll be with you from the adjuster visit to the final paperwork.

    General information, not legal or insurance advice. Coverage depends on your policy, and claim rules differ by state. Always read your policy and talk with your agent.
