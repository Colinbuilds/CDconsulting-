    Storm damage & insurance

    Claims, in plain English.

    How roof and exterior claims actually work, when filing makes sense, and where I fit in. No storm-chaser games.

    Usually worth a claim

    • Hail damage. Bruised or cracked shingles, dents in gutters, vents, downspouts and AC fins. Dented soft metals are often the first proof hail hit your property.
    • Wind damage. Missing, creased or lifted shingles, torn ridge cap, siding blown off.
    • A fallen tree or limb that damaged the roof, siding, gutters or windows.
    • A sudden leak from storm damage that's now staining ceilings or walls.
    • Damage well above your deductible, reported within your policy's deadline. Many give you a year or less.

    Think twice, or skip it

    • Age and wear. Curling, granule loss from age and a roof at the end of its life are maintenance, not covered damage.
    • Damage close to your deductible. A $2,400 repair on a $2,000 deductible pays little and still goes on your record.
    • Small repairs like a few blown-off shingles. Paying out of pocket protects your claims history.
    • Pre-existing or neglected issues such as old leaks, clogged gutters or failed flashing.
    • Several recent claims. They can raise premiums or affect renewal. Ask your agent.

    Should I file?

    Rough numbers in, a straight answer out. Your adjuster and policy make the real call.

    How a claim goes, start to finish

    1. 01

      Stay safe, document, protect

      Note the storm date and take photos from the ground. Tarp or move belongings to stop further damage, and keep the receipts. Reasonable emergency repairs are usually reimbursable.

      I'll tarp it and photograph everything.

    2. 02

      Free inspection before you file

      A professional checks the roof, gutters, soft metals, siding and windows, and tells you honestly whether it's storm damage above your deductible.

      A photo report and a straight answer, even if it's "don't file."

    3. 03

      You file the claim

      Call your insurer or use their app. Write down your claim number, adjuster and any deadlines.

      I'll walk you through what to say and send.

    4. 04

      Adjuster inspection

      The insurer sends an adjuster. Having your contractor there makes sure nothing is missed.

    Words on your paperwork

    Deductible

    What you pay before insurance pays. Wind/hail deductibles are often a percentage of your dwelling coverage: 2% of $350,000 is $7,000.

    RCV · replacement cost value

    Pays to replace with new materials. The first check usually holds back depreciation; you get it once the work is finished and invoiced.

    ACV · actual cash value

    Pays the depreciated value of your old roof. On an older roof that can be a fraction of the replacement cost.

    Recoverable depreciation

    The amount held back on an RCV policy. It's your money, but you have to finish the job and submit the final invoice, usually within a set deadline.

    Scope of loss

    The adjuster's line-item estimate of what's damaged and what it pays for. Read every line.

    Supplement

    A request to add items the original estimate missed, like drip edge, ice & water shield, starter strip or code-required work, backed by photos and measurements.

    Code upgrade · ordinance or law

    Optional coverage that pays for work current building code requires, even if your old roof didn't have it.

    Two-party check

    If you have a mortgage, checks are often made out to you and your lender, who has to endorse them before funds are released.

    What I do for you

    • Free storm inspection with a photo report
    • An honest file-or-don't recommendation
    • Meet your adjuster on site
    • A detailed line-item estimate for your claim

    What I won't do

    • Waive, absorb or "cover" your deductible. It's illegal in many states and can void a claim.
    • Negotiate your settlement. In most states only licensed public adjusters or attorneys can.
    • Ask you to sign over your insurance benefits on the spot.
    • Inflate damage or bill for work that wasn't done.

    Storm-chaser red flags

    • Door-knockers right after a storm with out-of-area plates
    • "Free roof" or "we'll cover your deductible"
    • Pressure to sign before an inspection or adjuster visit
    • Large cash deposits up front
    • No local address, license or references
